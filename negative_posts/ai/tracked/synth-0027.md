Controlled experiments in a game world — Iris Glen

The demonstration uses a Python 3D engine to study how people choose a route through an unfamiliar space. Participants see a game; researchers need repeatable trial conditions and accurate measurements. I want to hear how the team separates display timing from the timing of the participant's decision. This talk uses a catalogue application to explain an in-memory cache. The program promises to cover eviction, expiration, and the sudden load created when a popular key expires. We have been treating cache hits as a simple performance bonus. I suspect the failure cases will reveal some assumptions worth revisiting.

Reading a PostgreSQL query plan — Tessa Bloom

Instead of presenting a list of settings to copy, Tessa will walk through a slow reporting query and its execution plan. I hope to understand why a useful index can go unused and what changes when the planner's estimates are wrong. A larger database machine may buy time, but I would like a diagnosis first.

This session starts with a small invoice parser and adds tests around its observable behavior. The outline includes malformed input and dates at month boundaries. That is a reassuring level of specificity. People learning testing need to see what to assert, how to name a failure, and how to keep a test from depending on their laptop.
